End Spam Forever

Proof-of-work stamps for outgoing mail, verification for incoming mail.

An ESF stamp shows that a sender spent measurable computing time for this specific recipient. It is a scarce-resource signal, not authentication: it says nothing about who sent a message, or whether its content is safe. ESF complements SPF, DKIM and DMARC rather than replacing them.

Outgoing

Most sends finish inside this and you never notice ESF at all. Afterwards the search keeps running — the toolbar button just starts showing progress. With ESF enabled a message is meant to carry a stamp, so it is not abandoned here.

The emergency exit, reached only on a slow machine or a high difficulty. Work is exponential: at 18 bits a second is usually enough, 22 bits needs about fifteen. Measure my hash rate below shows what your machine actually manages.

A header field is visible to every recipient. Including a Bcc token lets that recipient verify their stamp, but someone who already suspects an address can test that guess against the token.

Incoming

Default: stamps do not expire — work stays work, and a result that silently turned red after a week would be confusing on stored mail. A window makes a sender keep spending instead of minting stamps for months on idle hardware and dumping them in one campaign; it is only worth setting if that matters to you. Either way, freshness is measured against the message's own date, so your archive never goes stale, and a stamp reused on a second message is still refused as a replay.

Valid stamps below this threshold stay yellow instead of green. Difficulty numbers are only comparable within one work profile.

Almost no mail carries a stamp today, so this will flag most of your inbox. During adoption, a missing stamp should not be treated as malicious.
